K-Fold Cross Validation Technique

K-Fold Validation is a technique used to measure the performance of a machine learning model by dividing
the datasets into K subsets of equal sizes, called folds. The model is then trained on K - 1 folds and tested on the
remaining fold. This is then repeated K times, with each fold being used as testing exactly once. Then the
performance of the model is averaged over all K iterations to get an accurate estimate of the model's performance.

Cross validation is used to:
    - Avoid overfitting
        - By ensuring that the model does not perform well only on the training data but also for unseen data.
    - Provide robust evaluation
        - This is done by averaging the results over multiple iterations, which reduces bias and variance in the
        performance estimates.
    - Efficiently use data
        - Maximizes the utilization of the dataset, especially in small datasets.

K-Fold Cross Validation Steps:
    1. Split data into K folds, dividing the dataset into K subsets or folds.
    2. Train-Test Iterations
        - Where for each fold uses K - 1 folds for training the model
        - And use the remaining fold to test the model and evaluate its performance.
    3. Aggregate Results
        - Calculate the performance of the fold and average the results of all iterations.
        (ex: accuracy, precision, recall, etc.)
